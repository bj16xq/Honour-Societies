## Background
* An honour society having focus groups is an effective way to engage members and support productive conversation that may lead to advocacy and the improvement of programs or policy.
* Theses are often the most important aspect of a research-based program.

**Note that the term "thesis" is used to encapsulate theses, dissertations, major research papers, and exegeses (for arts-based or capstone projects).**

## Content

### Discussion 1: "Challenge Mode" Thesis
**Introduction**\
This discussion is meant to facilitate contemplation of a hypothetical initiative called the "Challenge Mode" Thesis. The essence of this initiative is that thesis students are provided the option to, upon completing their thesis, create a special draft of their thesis by adding any combination of features that turn the original thesis into a "challenge mode" thesis. These features are meant to enrich the experience for the reader through adding challenge and gamification.\
\
Possible features for "challenge mode" are as follows:
* Vocabulary challenge: Replacing a portion of words with low-frequency words (i.e., difficult words) without altering meaning (e.g., boring → insipid). This adds challenge because readers may need to look up these words in a dictionary to understand the text. For this feature, one should target regular words rather than disciplinary terminology. This feature is ideal for a challenge mode thesis because in a regular thesis, using words that are unnecessarily difficult detracts from  accessibility.
* Comprehension questions for the reader in one of two forms: (i) after the entire thesis, which is better for shorter theses or exegeses; (ii) after each section/chapter, which is better for longer theses.
* Writing out examples of statistical formulae in the data analysis subsection of the method section with incomplete calculations such that the reader must use the formula to complete the calculation within the example.
* Bonus activities: Creating a noisy word search or crossword at the end of one's thesis using the thesis topic as the theme.
* Easter eggs: Adding thematic material in hidden ways, such as a hidden message deciphered by juxtaposing the first or last letter in a series of paragraphs (relevant sentences would need to be reworded to be able to create appropriate words).

**Preliminary Considerations**
* Establishing institutional policy may prompt the institution to: (a) communicate to thesis students about the option to make a challenge mode thesis; (b) provide the option for the challenge mode thesis to be posted alongside the main thesis on the institution's repository. These options probably entail passing through academic governance.
* Regardless of whether policy exists, students may always elect to undertake this task in an informal capacity and disseminate it with their own means (e.g., through ResearchGate).
* Students writing their own comprehension questions is beneficial for content mastery (Aflalo, 2021).
* Due to its project-based nature, this activity can promote students' higher-order thinking (Chen & Yang, 2019).

**Discussion Questions**
* What are the overall pros and cons to students undertaking this task?
* What are the pros and cons to establishing a policy on hosting a challenge mode thesis on an institution's repository?
* What are the pros and cons to the institutional practice of sharing with students the option of the challenge mode thesis, regardless of whether there is the option to post on the institution's repository?
* Might the discussion of challenge mode theses be a worthwhile topic for thesis/professional seminars?
